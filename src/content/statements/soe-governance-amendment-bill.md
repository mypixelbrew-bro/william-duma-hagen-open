Minister for State Enterprises, Hon. William Duma, has welcomed the passage of the SOE Governance Amendment Bill by the National Parliament, describing it as an essential step toward transparent and accountable management of state assets.

The bill, which passed with bipartisan support, codifies reforms that have been progressively introduced across the State-owned enterprise portfolio since 2022, including performance-based contracts for SOE chief executives, independent recruitment processes for board appointments, and mandatory public disclosure of SOE financial performance.

"This bill means these reforms do not depend on any one minister or any one government," Mr Duma told Parliament. "Independent boards, published accounts, and executive contracts tied to measurable outcomes — this is now the law, not just the practice of the current administration."

The bill applies to all major State-owned enterprises, including PNG Power, Air Niugini, PNG Ports Corporation and the Kumul Consolidated Holdings group. It establishes an independent SOE Performance Review Panel reporting to the Public Accounts Committee, and requires annual public disclosure of SOE financial performance.

Mr Duma said the legislation followed extensive consultation with SOE boards, industry stakeholders and development partners, and would come into effect immediately following ministerial gazettal.
